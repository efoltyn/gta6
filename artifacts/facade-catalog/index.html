Every facade,
Stone Age to near future.

Every grammar the kit has, in chronological order, photographed from three angles on the same shell within its size class. The eight eras the kit had no answer for were written for this sheet — and most of them were unbuildable until a facade could tell the shell to hand over the wall plane, because almost none of them have glazing.

Three-quarter

The honest single frame: entrance face and one flank at once, so ornament applied only to the front cannot hide.

Who owns the wall

A grammar now declares it. keep — the shell glazes as always, and its real furnished rooms show through. frame — punched masonry openings to set piers and arches around. own — solid wall, no glazing, and the culture’s own openings, or none at all.

From the pavement

Where a player actually meets the building. Judges the door, the reach, and whether it still reads from underneath.

Rear three-quarter

The cheat detector: a facade that spent everything on the door elevation looks fine in the other two frames and empty here. On tower plates this frame is the crown instead — a tower wraps all four faces by construction, and what it lives on is how it ends.
